# Sources

Research notes behind the map. Every site panel in the app links back to the entries here.
Where sources disagree, or a claim rests on a single source, that is recorded here rather than resolved silently.
The map was independently fact-checked once; corrections from that check are marked **Corrected**.

Accessed November 2026.

## 1. Anemurium
- Wikipedia, "Anemurium" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemurium
- Turkish Museums (Ministry of Culture and Tourism), "Mersin Anemurium Archaeological Site" — https://turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/2158-mersin-anamorium-archaeological-site/2158/4
- Livius.org, "Anemurium (Anamur)" — https://livius.org/articles/place/anemurium-anamur
- Hürriyet Daily News, "Mersin's Anemurium ancient city aims for UNESCO recognition" — https://hurriyetdailynews.com/mersins-anemurium-ancient-city-aims-for-unesco-recognition-194182
- Underlying scholarship: Canadian excavations (E. Alföldi-Rosenbaum; J. Russell, Univ. of British Columbia), final publication vol. 1, 2021.
- **Used:** large theatre + small odeon/bouleuterion; three large baths and one small; palaestra mosaic; necropolis of ~350 tombs, 1st–4th c. AD; abandonment around the 7th century.
- **Not used:** a 900-seat figure for the odeon and an earthquake "c. 580" appear in secondary web sources only; they were dropped from the panel.

## 2. Seleucia ad Calycadnum (Silifke)
- Wikipedia, "Silifke" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silifke
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Seleucia Tracheotis" — https://www.britannica.com/place/Seleucia-Tracheotis
- Vici.org entries for the temple and bridge — https://vici.org/vici/30497/
- **Used:** founded by Seleucus I Nicator (c. 296–280 BC) with settlers from Holmi; "renowned 2nd-century Temple of Jupiter"; stone bridge built AD 77 by governor L. Octavius Memor; rock-cut cistern Tekir Ambarı; rock-cut necropolis; Aya Thekla.
- Wikipedia, "Temple of Jupiter (Silifke)" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Silifke) — peripteros of 8 × 14 Corinthian columns, c. 40 × 21 m; one column survives; later a church.
- Turkey Photo Guide, "Silifke" — https://turkeyphotoguide.com/silifke — storks on the surviving column, "Temple of Storks".
- **Caveat:** the temple's dedication is traditional. Turkish Archaeological News argues for a Seleucid-period origin rather than the 2nd c. AD. The bridge has been heavily rebuilt.

## 3. Diocaesarea (Uzuncaburç)
- Wikipedia, "Uzuncaburç (Diokaisareia)" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzuncabur%C3%A7_(Diokaisareia)
- Turkish Museums, "Mersin Uzuncaburç (Diokaisareia) Archaeological Site" — https://turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/2167-mersin-uzuncaburc-diokaisareia-archaeological-site/2167/4
- Livius.org, "Olba / Diocaesarea" — https://livius.org/articles/place/olba-diocaesarea
- Lonely Planet, "Uzuncaburç Ruins" (theatre capacity) — https://www.lonelyplanet.com/turkey/uzuncaburc/attractions/uzuncaburc-ruins/a/poi-sig/1250521/1324932
- **Used:** Hellenistic peripteral Temple of Zeus Olbios, converted to a church in the 5th c.; Temple of Tyche (1st c. AD, donors Oppius and Kyria); theatre under Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus (161–169); city status under Vespasian; monumental gate; tall Hellenistic tower.
- **Discrepancy:** standing columns of the Zeus temple are given as "36" in one source although a 6 × 12 peripteros has 32 — the panel says "some thirty". Tyche temple date: "1st century AD" vs. "reign of Vespasian or Domitian" (compatible). The 2,500-seat figure is from a guidebook only. **Corrected:** an earlier draft gave AD 72 for city status. The sources cited here (Wikipedia, Livius) say only "under Vespasian", and the Ministry page says only that it became an autonomous city in the Roman period, so the panel gives no year ("c. AD 72" appears in the Princeton Encyclopedia of Classical Sites tradition, not consulted directly). The 5th-century date for the church conversion is from the Ministry page. The tower's number of storeys is not in these sources and is not stated.

- Wikipedia, "Corycus" (ancient authors on the cave) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corycus

- **Used:** Cennet 250 × 110 m, c. 70 m deep, ~300 steps; Cehennem 70 × 50 m, 128 m deep; Typhon myth; saffron (Strabo); 5th-c. chapel of the Virgin built by one Paulus; temple of Zeus on the rim later turned into a church.

## 5. Adamkayalar
- Wikipedia, "Adamkayalar" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamkayalar
- Daily Sabah, "Ancient site 'Men of Rock' in Anatolia unveils unique reliefs" — https://www.dailysabah.com/arts/ancient-site-men-of-rock-in-anatolia-unveils-unique-reliefs/news — feasts for the dead; two male figures shown as soldiers.
- **Used:** 18 figures in nine niches (eleven men, four women, two children, one ibex), two eagles on a pediment; "probably 2nd century AD"; 7 km from Kızkalesi; damaged by treasure hunters in 2019.

## 6. Corycus (Kızkalesi)
- Wikipedia, "Corycus" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corycus (drawing on R. W. Edwards' survey of the castles, 1982/1987)
- **Corrected:** an earlier draft placed the naval Battle of Corycus (191 BC) here, following the Wikipedia "Corycus" article. That battle was fought off Corycus in Ionia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Corycus — and has been removed.
- **Used:** Roman fleet station and mint; arch, necropolis, sarcophagi, churches; island castle re-fortified in the early 12th c. under Alexios I Komnenos; Armenian inscriptions of Levon I and Hetum I; land castle built of spolia from the Roman city.
- **Caveat:** the castles are medieval. They are modelled because they are the landmark, and labelled as medieval in the panel.

## 7. Elaiussa Sebaste (Ayaş)
- Sapienza Università di Roma, Dip. di Scienze dell'Antichità, "Elaiussa Sebaste — The theatre" — https://www.antichita.uniroma1.it/eng/node/521
- E. Borgia, "Il teatro di Elaiussa Sebaste", *Dionysus ex Machina* 1 (2010), via Vici.org — https://vici.org/vici/17238/
- Wikipedia, "Elaiussa Sebaste" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaiussa_Sebaste
- **Used:** theatre excavated 1995–1999; begun first half of 2nd c., completed 161–169; plan more than a half-circle, 55 m diameter, c. 2,300 spectators; partly ruined by c. AD 300. City refounded by Archelaus of Cappadocia under Augustus as *Sebaste*; temple of 6 × 12 columns; aqueducts from the Lamos; necropolis of ~100 tombs; decline after Shapur I's raid of 260. Excavation director: Eugenia Equini Schneider.
- **Discrepancy:** Wikipedia calls the theatre "very small, 23 rows"; the excavators' figures are preferred.

## 8. Canytelis (Kanlıdivane)
- Wikipedia, "Kanlıdivane" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanl%C4%B1divane
- Turkish Museums, "Mersin Kanlı Divane (Kanytelleis) Archaeological Site" — https://turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/2163-mersin-kanli-divane-kanytelleis-archaeological-site/2163/4
- Showcaves.com, "Kanlıdivane" — https://showcaves.com/english/tr/karst/Kanlidivane.html
- **Used:** sinkhole c. 130 m on the long axis; tower inscription, quoted in Wikipedia's reading: "Built by Teukros, the son of priest king Tarkyaris of Olba for Zeus"; mausoleum built by Aba for her husband and sons; basilicas; renamed Neapolis under Theodosius II.
- **Discrepancy:** size given as 130 m on the long axis (Wikipedia) and 170 × 200 m (Ministry page); depth as 60 m and as about 70 m. The panel and narration say "some 70 metres" and note the range. The source says Aba built the tomb for her husband and "sons" (no number).

## 9. Soli-Pompeiopolis
- Wikipedia, "Soli, Cilicia" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soli,_Cilicia
- Hürriyet Daily News, "Roman streets of ancient Soli gradually unearthed" — https://hurriyetdailynews.com/roman-streets-of-ancient-soli-gradually-unearthed--85266
- Hürriyet Daily News, "Ancient harbor comes to surface" — https://hurriyetdailynews.com/ancient-harbor-comes-to-surface-175934
- Hürriyet Daily News, "Aratos' mausoleum searched in Soli Pompeiopolis" — https://hurriyetdailynews.com/aratos-mausoleum-searched-in-soli-pompeiopolis-167043
- DergiPark article on the colonnaded street — https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/316007
- Küre Ansiklopedi, "Soli Pompeipolis Ancient City" — https://kureansiklopedi.com/detay/soli-pompeipolis-ancient-city-3623c
- ROMACONS harbour-concrete coring campaign, 2009 (cited in the Wikipedia article).
- **Used:** Rhodian/Achaean colony c. 700 BC; depopulated by Tigranes 83 BC; refounded by Pompey with ex-pirates after 67 BC; harbour in Roman concrete, renovated c. AD 130; Aratus and Chrysippus born here; earthquake AD 525; excavations since 1999 under Remzi Yağcı.
- **Discrepancy:** street length 450 m (journal article) vs. ~350 m (encyclopedia); width 14.5 m in both. Columns standing: 33, 42 or 47 of ~200, depending on source and year (columns are still being re-erected). The panel gives the ranges.

## 10. Tarsus
- Wikipedia, "Tarsus, Mersin" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsus,_Mersin
- T.C. Ministry of Culture and Tourism, "Tarsus — Where to visit" — https://ktb.gov.tr/EN-36260/where-to-visit.html
- Wikipedia, "Donuktaş" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donukta%C5%9F
- World History Encyclopedia, "Tarsus" — https://www.worldhistory.org/Tarsus
- Plutarch, *Life of Antony* 26 (Cleopatra's barge).
- **Caveat:** Julian was buried outside the walls of Tarsus in 363; whether his remains were later moved to Constantinople is disputed, so the narration says only that he "was buried here".
- **Used:** capital of the province of Cilicia from 67 BC; Antony and Cleopatra, 41 BC; birthplace of Paul (Acts 21:39); Julian buried here, AD 363; Cleopatra's Gate; basalt-paved street in the centre; Donuktaş; Roman road at Sağlıklı (c. 3 km, 2.94–3.00 m wide).
- **Discrepancy:** Cleopatra's Gate is popularly Roman but its present fabric is attributed to the Byzantine or Abbasid period and was heavily restored in 1994. Donuktaş: 2nd-c. AD Roman temple (excavators; possibly under Commodus) vs. older "tomb of Sardanapalus" tradition.

## Map data
- **Elevation:** Copernicus GLO-30 Digital Elevation Model (30 m) — https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/ — © DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018, provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved. `tools/build_terrain_data.py` averages it to a 1024 × 702 mesh grid (~260 m) and to a 4096 × 2809 map of surface normals (~65 m) that carries the finer ridges and valleys in the lighting. Vertical exaggeration ×2. A terrace about 1.3 km across is levelled under each site so its model can sit at ground level.
- **Ground colour:** Sentinel-2 cloudless 2017 — https://s2maps.eu by EOX IT Services GmbH (Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2017), CC BY 4.0. Zoom-11 tiles resampled to a 4096 × 2809 colour map (~65 m), lightly desaturated. No lighting is added to it beyond what the summer mosaic already contains.
- **Land cover and trees:** ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 v200 — https://esa-worldcover.org — © ESA WorldCover project 2021 / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2021) processed by ESA WorldCover consortium, CC BY 4.0. It supplies the land/water boundary (the delta plains lie too low for the DEM to separate from the sea) and the placement of the 3D trees: where it maps tree cover (28 % of the land in this frame), shrubland or cropland. Species are assigned by the usual altitude belts of the Cilician Taurus (Turkish red pine low, black pine, then Taurus cedar and Cilician fir, juniper to the treeline; olive and carob among lowland fields and for tree cover on the plains) — a rule of thumb, not a forest inventory.
- **Back-dating:** this is today's landscape, not a reconstruction of the Roman one. Built-up areas, dam reservoirs and greenhouse sheeting are painted over with the colour of the farmland and scrub around them; the Göksu and Tarsus delta lagoons are kept. Modern field patterns, plantations, cleared forest and the present coastline are not corrected.

- **Rivers** (Calycadnus/Göksu, Cydnus/Berdan, Lamos/Limonlu) are traced by `tools/trace_rivers.py` as least-elevation paths down the DEM's valley floors from a point on each river, through the ancient city on it (Seleucia, Tarsus), to the sea. They follow the real valleys at ~260 m resolution but are not surveyed centrelines, and the lower Göksu ends at the delta lagoon.
- **Roman roads** are schematic: splines through attested stations (Anemurium – Kelenderis – Seleucia – Corycus – Elaiussa – Soli – Tarsus; Seleucia – Diocaesarea; Tarsus – Sağlıklı – Cilician Gates). They show connections, not surveyed alignments.
- **Monument models** are procedural miniatures composed from the standing remains listed above; not to scale.

## Sound
- Narration, ambience, effects and music were generated with the ElevenLabs API (`tools/generate_audio.py`): text-to-speech model `eleven_v4` (the generator logs the model used per clip and every clip ran on it), voice "George"; sound effects and music via the sound-generation and music endpoints; levels and the music loop point were then adjusted with ffmpeg (`tools/postprocess_audio.sh`). Narration scripts are in `src/narration.json` and use only facts listed above.
